# Ultimate Beginner's Guide to EVE Online — Multiboxing Content Framework

> **Framework version:** 1.4  
> **Created:** August 18, 2026  
> **Status:** Governing project source  
> **Applies to:** Every section, career path, skill plan, fit, economic model, visual plan, and revision involving alts, MCT, multiple accounts, or simultaneous clients

---

## 1. Governing Decision

The guide must intentionally lead readers from single-character fundamentals toward effective use of alternate characters, specialized accounts, Multiple Character Training, and legal multiboxing.

Multiboxing is not a disposable sidebar or a subject introduced for the first time in the veteran material. It is a major progression path because it can:

- Increase a player's value to a corporation or alliance
- Scale Planetary Industry, manufacturing, research, reactions, trade, hauling, and resource harvesting
- Combine miners, boosts, compression, hauling, scouting, logistics, and other complementary roles
- Reduce dependence on finding another player for every supporting task
- Expand the range of EVE systems a player can operate together
- Create advanced planning, logistics, and organizational gameplay that a single active character cannot reproduce

The guide begins with one character because a reader must understand each client independently before managing several. That foundation must lead somewhere: the intended long-term progression includes multi-character and multi-account operations when they provide meaningful career, corporation, economic, or strategic value.

The guide must not claim that a specific account count guarantees fun or profit. It should state clearly that single-character play imposes important limits for many industrial, logistical, and fleet-support goals, then teach readers how to scale deliberately.

## 2. Required Terminology

`glossary.md` is the canonical source for the definitions of character, main, alt, account, Alpha, Omega, MCT, client, multiboxing, input broadcasting, and account architecture. Use those terms precisely and never interchange them for convenience.

This framework adds two non-negotiable interpretation rules:

- MCT enables another training queue on the same account; it does not enable same-account characters to log in together.
- When intended roles must operate simultaneously, explain the need for separate eligible accounts under current official rules.

## 3. The Progression Model

Teach account growth in this order:

1. **One-character competence:** Learn controls, fitting, survival, skill planning, income, and corporation participation.
2. **Role identification:** Find a recurring bottleneck, income opportunity, or corporation service role.
3. **First alt decision:** Decide whether the role belongs on the existing account or requires a separate account.
4. **Minimum viable specialization:** Train only enough to make the new role useful before pursuing long level-V plans.
5. **Two-client operation:** Learn window organization, fleet setup, movement, broadcasts, independent inputs, attention limits, and operational resilience.
6. **Career scaling:** Add complementary roles for mining, PI, industry, hauling, trade, exploration, PvE, PvP, or dangerous-space logistics.
7. **Operational portfolio:** Optimize account costs, MCT timing, training queues, capital, characters, security, and corporation value.
8. **Veteran rationalization:** Consolidate, transfer, pause, or retire roles that no longer justify their cost or attention.

Do not recommend creating accounts merely to fill empty character-name ideas. Every proposed character must have a stated purpose, login relationship, training plan, economic or social value, and stopping point.

## 4. Character, MCT, or Additional Omega Decision

Use this foundational decision logic throughout the guide and in the **Character and Account Value Advisor**:

1. **Must the new role be logged in at the same time as an existing role?**
   - Yes: use a separate Omega account.
   - No: continue to the next question.
2. **Does the role need meaningful training soon?**
   - Yes: compare temporary MCT with training it on a separate account.
   - No: place it on an available same-account character slot and train it when the main queue can pause.
3. **Will the role eventually become simultaneous?**
   - Yes: consider creating it on the intended long-term separate account from the beginning.
   - No: same-account specialization may be the cheaper structure.
4. **How often and for how long will the role be useful?**
   - Distinguish one-time, occasional, most-session, temporary, seasonal, and permanent needs.
5. **Does the role's attention pattern fit the player's preferred experience?**
   - Classify the role as passive, periodic, continuous, split-focus, or high-pressure. Compare that demand with relaxed, active, challenging, or maximum-efficiency play.
6. **Is the cost comfortable for the stated goal?**
   - Use full financial analysis for income or self-funding goals, a shorter cost check for operational goals, and an affordability check for fun, variety, or flexibility goals.

Do not ask for detailed corporation-support, collaboration, or independence preferences as mandatory inputs. The result may briefly remind the reader that corporation services or another player can change the decision.

### Character and Account Value Advisor standard

The advisor is anonymous and advisory. It does not use login, ESI, saved account data, or assumed knowledge of the visitor's skills. Give every input concise explanatory help and base readiness statements on the guide progression and linked fit or activity.

Before implementation, create and approve `interactive-tools-specification.md` under the boundaries in `site-product-specification.md`. The implementation specification must preserve the decision logic and output requirements below, process inputs locally by default, define accessible behavior and test cases, and must not expand the approved tool count.

Compare five realistic options:

1. Continue the current character.
2. Add a same-account character.
3. Use temporary MCT.
4. Add a separate Omega account.
5. Use corporation services or another player.

Return one primary recommendation and one alternative. Use one of these non-shaming conclusions when helpful: **Financially self-supporting**, **Strategically useful but not self-supporting**, **Worth considering mainly for fun or flexibility**, or **Probably premature for the stated goal**.

Every result explains:

- Why the option fits the stated goal
- What it **unlocks**, **improves**, or merely **adds convenience**
- Costs and commitments
- Attention and enjoyment impact
- Main drawback
- Qualitative **Fun Value**, including who may enjoy it and what may become frustrating
- What could change the recommendation
- The best next guide step

Present economic, operational, and personal value separately. Never fabricate a combined score. The mining boost and compression account is the reference use case because simultaneous operation, meaningful corporation value, economics, attention, and enjoyment all affect the answer.

### Common architecture patterns

| Goal | Likely architecture | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| PI colonies, research, manufacturing, or market roles used at different times | Same-account characters, with temporary MCT when useful | Adds specialized capacity without requiring simultaneous clients |
| Two miners operating together | Two Omega accounts | Both characters must be logged in simultaneously |
| Miners plus booster, compression, hauler, or scout | Separate Omega accounts for concurrent roles | Each role must remain active with the mining fleet |
| Blueprint, invention, reaction, manufacturing, trade, and hauling chain | Mixed architecture | Asynchronous specialists may share accounts; concurrent transport or support may require separate accounts |
| Dangerous-space travel, eyes, cyno support where applicable, or rescue | Separate Omega support account | Support must often be online while the main character moves or operates |

These are starting patterns, not universal prescriptions. Current mechanics, corporation infrastructure, player attention, and subscription economics may change the answer.

## 7. Economic Standard

Use this formula for every scaling recommendation:

> **Incremental net value = additional revenue − Omega or MCT cost − taxes and fees − hauling and fuel − expected losses − capital opportunity cost**

Also evaluate:

- ISK per active hour
- ISK per attention hour
- ISK per account-month
- Break-even time
- Startup and replacement capital
- Assets exposed in space
- Market and sales bottlenecks
- Hardware and client-performance requirements
- Cognitive load and error rate
- Corporation reimbursement, infrastructure, or service benefits
- Enjoyment and burnout risk

For reader-facing account advice, present three distinct dimensions:

- **Economic value:** costs, incremental profit, break-even, and capital at risk
- **Operational value:** simultaneous roles, independence, reduced waiting, corporation usefulness, and access
- **Personal value:** enjoyment, variety, attention, hardware burden, stress, and burnout

The correct question is not “How much can all clients earn?” It is “What additional net value does the next character or account create, and what new bottleneck appears after it is added?”

Treat PI and installed industry jobs as **asynchronous**, not literally passive. Treat mining as active, attention-limited multiboxing. Explain the difference because it determines whether same-account characters or simultaneous Omega accounts are appropriate.

## 8. Legal, Technical, and Security Standard

Before publishing any relevant procedure, research current official rules for:

- Alpha and Omega concurrent-client limits
- Multiple Character Training
- Manual multiboxing
- Input broadcasting or input duplication
- Macros, bots, and gameplay automation
- Account sharing
- Character transfers
- Launcher account management
- EVE SSO and third-party permissions

The guide must never recommend input broadcasting, duplicated actions, botting, gameplay automation, or account sharing. Each active client must be controlled independently.

Teach:

- Unique account credentials and two-factor authentication
- A secure password manager
- Launcher organization and recognizable account labels
- Legitimate EVE SSO and scope review
- Periodic removal of unused third-party permissions
- Recovery information and email security
- Corporation-role separation and least-privilege access
- Window layouts, hotkeys, client profiles, and hardware settings that reduce mistakes without automating gameplay

## 9. Visual Requirements

The multiboxing path requires more than screenshots. Use exact diagrams and decision visuals where relationships or scaling are difficult to explain linearly.

| Priority | Visual | Teaching purpose |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Account–character–queue topology | Show accounts, character slots, MCT queues, clone state, and simultaneous-login boundaries |
| 2 | MCT versus additional Omega decision tree | Help readers choose the correct account structure |
| 3 | Legal manual-control boundary | Distinguish window management from prohibited duplicated input or automation |
| 4 | Alt-role and skill matrix | Compare role, account placement, training priority, and simultaneous-login requirement |
| 5 | PI network diagram | Show extraction, factories, customs offices, hauling, markets, and taxes |
| 6 | Industry supply-chain diagram | Show research, copying, invention, reactions, production, staging, hauling, and sales |
| 7 | Mining fleet layouts | Show recommended responsibilities at one, two, three-to-five, and larger client counts |
| 8 | Break-even and diminishing-returns chart | Show incremental profit and the bottleneck created by each added account |
| 9 | Attention heatmap | Show which roles demand continuous input and which can be checked intermittently |
| 10 | Account security and permission map | Show accounts, email, authentication, SSO scopes, corporation roles, and assets |

All diagrams must include a plain-language explanation and remain readable to someone who has never multiboxed.

## 10. Blueprint and Review Checklist

Before approving a section blueprint, confirm:

- [ ] Multiboxing coverage is marked foundational, applicable, advanced, or not materially relevant.
- [ ] Applicable roles are integrated into the section's main progression.
- [ ] Character, account, MCT, Omega, and active-client assumptions are explicit.
- [ ] The reader learns why and when to add a role, not merely how to create an account.
- [ ] Same-account and separate-account options are compared where both are viable.
- [ ] Minimum viable skill plans and stopping points are assigned.
- [ ] Net-value and break-even assumptions are assigned for research.
- [ ] Attention, hardware, safety, security, and operational resilience are covered.
- [ ] Current official policy research is assigned.
- [ ] Required visuals are identified.
- [ ] Corporation or alliance services that alter the recommendation are acknowledged.
- [ ] The material does not imply that more accounts always produce more profit.
- [ ] The material does not postpone all meaningful multiboxing guidance until Section 17.
